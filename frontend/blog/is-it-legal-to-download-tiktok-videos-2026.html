Is It Legal to Download TikTok Videos? What TikTok's Own Policies Say (2026)

This page is not legal advice, and it cannot decide a specific case. “Is it legal to download a TikTok?” is not one yes-or-no for every file. It depends on whose post it is, what you do with the copy, whether the post includes music you do not own, the creator’s Platform settings, and which TikTok Terms of Service apply where you live. TikTok publishes separate Terms for the United States (last updated July 15, 2026); for the European Economic Area, Switzerland, and the United Kingdom (last updated July 2026); and for other regions (last updated 1 December 2025). Those other-region Terms apply if you are not in the US, EEA, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, or India; if you are in India, use the Terms TikTok shows for that region — this guide does not quote an India-specific page. TikTok also publishes a global Intellectual Property Policy (released March 27, 2025, effective April 26, 2025). Those documents describe user licenses, download-related settings, and copyright. They do not turn a third-party website into blanket permission to republish someone else’s work. Keep three situations apart: a personal copy of a public post, a backup of a video you created, and any reuse, repost, or commercial use. If you save a public TikTok as a file, paste its complete public http(s) link into DLTK (https://dltk.io/) and wait until the browser finishes writing the file. DLTK cannot download private or friends-only posts. Respect copyright. Do not republish someone else’s video or music without permission.

What TikTok’s Own Policies Actually Say

This section maps official public pages; it is not a verdict. Read the Terms that apply to you — the wording is not identical everywhere, and TikTok updates these documents. The dates above are the “last updated” or effective dates as of this writing (November 2026). If a live page has changed, the live page controls.

US Terms, section 3.5: as between you and TikTok, you own Your Content, except where the Terms say otherwise for TikTok Content. Depending on your Platform usage, including your sharing activities and settings, you also grant each user a non-exclusive, royalty-free, perpetual and irrevocable, worldwide license to use Your Content, including to copy, share, or download, in accordance with those Terms.

EEA, Switzerland, and UK Terms, section 4.9: TikTok does not own your content if you own the intellectual property rights in it. You grant each user a non-exclusive, royalty-free, worldwide licence to access and use your content, including to copy, share, or download, using the Platform for entertainment purposes, subject to your Platform settings. Those licences end when you close your account or when the content is removed, except that a licence can continue if you allowed reuse via Platform settings (the Terms give Duet, Stitch, download, or share as examples), or if TikTok must store or process the content for legal reasons.

Other-region Terms, section 7: TikTok Content may not be downloaded or otherwise exploited without prior written consent from TikTok or, where applicable, its licensors. You or the owner still own the copyright in User Content you submit, but by submitting it you grant TikTok a licence that includes authorising other users and other third parties to view, access, use, and download that User Content. Those Terms also say the Services are provided for private, non-commercial use, and they grant you a limited, revocable licence to access TikTok Content solely for personal, non-commercial use through your use of the Services.

A licence “to download” in a platform contract is not, by itself, permission to repost the video, put it in an advertisement, or sell it. If you need that kind of right, get it from the rights holder, or take advice. These summaries do not replace the full section that applies to you.

The Intellectual Property Policy says TikTok does not allow content that infringes copyright, and that using someone else’s copyrighted content without proper authorization or a legally valid reason may violate TikTok’s policies. It also notes that not every unauthorized use is an infringement: many countries have exceptions, including fair use in the United States, fair dealing in the European Union, and equivalent local rules. This guide does not decide whether any exception applies to your download or to a later use of the file. The policy describes removal of infringing content, a repeat-infringer policy that can lead to account bans, and a copyright report form. The US and EEA Terms both tell users to read that policy and point to the same report links.

Music is often a separate right. The other-region Terms state, in capital letters, that no rights are licensed with respect to sound recordings and the musical works embodied therein that are made available from or through the service. The US Terms list separate Music Terms for posts that include library or device music. Saving a file that contains a commercial track is not a music licence, and extracting the sound does not create one.

Official download settings are a control, not a copyright verdict. TikTok’s Download content help describes saving a video or photo when the creator allows others to download the content, and it points to the setting that manages whether others can download your posts. If Save video does not appear, the in-app control is off. That is the creator’s download setting — not a finding that a public post has no copyright, and not a finding that a third-party tool is forbidden or permitted. Method 2: Use TikTok’s Official Save Video When the Creator Allows It

Best for: A personal copy from inside the official app, when Save video is available on that post

  1. Open the public post in the TikTok app. Official help starts from the post itself, not a profile page.
  2. Tap Share, then Save video (or Save photo). It appears when the creator has allowed downloads. If it is missing, do not force the in-app control. A public post can still use Method 1. For a private or friends-only post, stop.
  3. Use the file only in a way you have a right to use it. Official Save does not, by itself, authorize a repost or a commercial use.

✅ Pros:

Method 3: Back Up a Video You Created

Best for: Keeping a copy of your own work, before or after you post it

The US and EEA Terms both say that, as between you and TikTok, you own the content you create, except for TikTok Content and unless the Terms say otherwise. That is about your own work. It does not cover a commercial sound you added, or a clip you stitched in from someone else.

  1. Before you post, use TikTok’s official save-to-device option if the posting screen offers it. Download content help recommends an offline copy of your own video or photos before you publish.
  2. After you post, use Share → Save video on your own public post, or paste that public link into DLTK.
  3. Store the file somewhere you control. Music or another person’s clip in the post may still belong to someone else.

✅ Pros:

  • This starts from work you created, the cleanest of the three situations

❌ Cons:

  • Music, effects, or stitched material can still belong to someone else. A backup is not a licence to reuse that sound or clip commercially

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it legal to download a TikTok just to watch it later?

There is no single official sentence that answers every personal-copy question for every region. The Terms that apply to you describe user licenses and, in some versions, personal or non-commercial use of the Services. The Intellectual Property Policy still requires respect for copyright. This page does not certify that your personal copy is lawful.

If the Terms mention a licence to download, can I repost the video?

Not on the basis of this guide. Download in the US and EEA examples is qualified by Platform settings or by use on the Platform. That is not permission to publish the file on another site, in an ad, or as your own product. Ask the rights holder when you want to reuse the work.

The creator turned off Save video. Does that make a browser download illegal?

A missing Save video button means the creator is not allowing in-app downloads. That setting is not a court ruling. A public post can still be pasted into DLTK. Copyright, the Terms, and permission for later reuse still apply. Private or friends-only posts work on neither route.

Can I download private or friends-only TikToks?

No. DLTK cannot download private or friends-only posts. Official Save is also not a way around those privacy settings. Do not look for a workaround.

What about the music in the TikTok?

Treat the sound as a separate right unless you know you own it. The other-region Terms state that no rights are licensed in the sound recordings and musical works made available from or through the service. The US Terms point to additional Music Terms. Extracting an MP3 does not create a music licence. The MP3 guide covers file steps only, not legal permission.

Does fair use or fair dealing automatically cover my download?

No. The Intellectual Property Policy lists those exceptions as examples of local law, and it also warns that unauthorized use may violate TikTok’s policies. Whether an exception applies is a fact-specific legal question this page does not answer.

Is using DLTK instead of the official button illegal by itself?

This guide does not make that blanket claim, and it does not make the opposite claim either. DLTK is a website that accepts a public TikTok post link. The official texts linked above do not mention DLTK by name. Your use of the file — personal copy, backup of your own work, or reuse — matters more than which button wrote the file.

How do I report a copyright problem on TikTok?

Use TikTok’s copyright report form, which the Intellectual Property Policy and the US and EEA Terms both point to. The policy encourages contacting the user first when that can resolve the issue, and it warns against submitting a misleading or fraudulent report.
